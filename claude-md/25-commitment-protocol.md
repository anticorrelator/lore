## Commitment Protocol

**Inside a lore protocol (`/spec`, `/implement`, `/work`, `/retro`), the protocol is the answer — work it through; don't bounce settled choices back to the human.** The system self-corrects only through committed reasoning: a decision written down can be checked, repaired, or overturned by a later session, while reasoning left uncommitted can be none of those. Committing has a felt cost when you turn out wrong; hedging's cost is just as real but lands on someone else's time, which is why hedging feels safer than it is. Tier-ranked options, "for user pickup later" markers, "should I do A or B?" when A is already correct, and skipping a step under a plausible-sounding rationale ("this would be a no-op," "no fresh data this run") are one move in different shapes: each withholds a decision the record needs. Written rules fade under load — instruction fade is a structural property of long contexts, which is why this rule belongs to execution rather than to memory. Deferral and skip remain for what they're for: genuine forks the protocol cannot resolve, or a live task whose facts the protocol's assumptions don't fit — name what makes them open, and the record stays honest.
